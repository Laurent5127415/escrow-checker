What does over-appraised mean?

The county thinks your home is worth more than it would sell for.

The appraisal district sets a "market value" for your home every January 1. In Tarrant County, that is the Tarrant Appraisal District (TAD).

Your tax bill = that value (minus exemptions) × the tax rates of each taxing unit.

If the value is higher than what your home would really sell for, your taxes are too high.

Signs your value is too high

  • It's well above what you paid recently
  • It's above your bank appraisal
  • It's above similar homes nearby
Quick test: county value ÷ what you paid (if you bought in the last year or two)
ResultWhat it means
1.10× or moreWorth a protest.
1.25× or more On your homesteadYou may qualify for a late correction (Form 50-869), even after the protest deadline.

What are taxing units?

Several local governments tax your home. One lower value lowers every one of them.

Each local government that taxes your home is a "taxing unit."

Each one sets its own rate. Your bill is the total of all of them.

The school district is usually the biggest share.

A typical Fort Worth home has six

  • City of Fort Worth
  • Tarrant County
  • Tarrant County Hospital District (JPS)
  • Tarrant County College
  • Tarrant Regional Water District
  • Fort Worth ISD, the school district

Your options: four ways to pay what's fair.

Free to file

Protest your value

The main way. Free to file.

Who
anyone who thinks the county value is too high.
Deadline
May 15, or 30 days after the appraisal district mails your notice, whichever is later.
Form
50-132, Notice of Protest.
How it goes
ask for an informal meeting with the appraisal district first. If it doesn't settle, you get a hearing before the Appraisal Review Board (ARB): in person, by phone or video, or by written affidavit.

Homestead exemption

Lowers the taxable value for school and other taxes.

Who
you, if you live in the home.
Deadline
usually April 30. Late applications are often allowed.

Form 50-869 motion to correct

A late fix under Tax Code 25.25(d).

Who
a homestead valued at least one-fourth (25%) higher than its correct value. Not allowed if you already protested that year's value.
Deadline
before the taxes become delinquent (before Feb 1). Pay the undisputed part of the tax by then.
Form

Check your escrow

Make sure your lender collects the right amount.

Who
anyone with an escrow account. Your servicer must send an annual escrow analysis.
What to check
tax and insurance amounts match real bills, and the cushion is no more than 2 months (1/6 of a year's escrow), under federal RESPA rules.

How to win a protest

Bring evidence. The Texas Comptroller suggests these.

  • Your closing statement or purchase priceShows what a buyer really paid
  • A recent appraisalFrom a bank or a licensed appraiser
  • Photos of problemsYours and the comparable homes
  • Repair estimates or receiptsPuts a dollar amount on problems
  • Sales of similar homes nearbySame size, age and area
  • A short written summary of your valueYour number, and why

Questions people ask.

Is HomeTally a law firm or a tax consultant?

No. HomeTally is not a law firm or a licensed property tax consultant; the Fix Kit is a do-it-yourself tool. You review, sign and file everything yourself.

What does the $39 Fix Kit include?

A filled Form 50-869 motion, a protest evidence packet, a homestead exemption guide (Form 50-114), an escrow dispute letter to your servicer and a deadline calendar, all made on your device. See the Fix Kit.

Does this file anything for me?

No. It prepares and explains. You file.

Is this legal or tax advice?

No. It's education.

Do I need to live in Tarrant County?

The county value lookup works for Tarrant County. The escrow check works anywhere in the US.

Will protesting raise my value?

The ARB decides based on the evidence. Protests rarely backfire, but bring evidence.

What if I missed May 15?

See Form 50-869 if your homestead is valued 25% or more over its correct value. You can also ask for a late protest for good cause.

Can a company protest for me?

Yes. Licensed property tax consultants or attorneys can, using Form 50-162. They usually charge a share of your savings. HomeTally's own done-for-you protest (about 15% of savings) is coming soon: join the waitlist.
